Is your business legible to AI search?

Answer ten questions about your own setup and get an instant score against the five factors that decide whether an AI assistant names you — plus what to fix first.

What this is and isn’t. This scores what you tell it, in your browser. Your answers stay there unless you choose to email yourself the plan at the end. It does not query ChatGPT or Perplexity about you — that’s the free visibility check, which a person runs. Both are free; they answer different questions.

0 of 10 answered0%
  1. 01Do your main service pages answer the core question in the first two sentences?

    Before any history, philosophy, or welcome message. If a page opens with three paragraphs about your practice, there is nothing at the top for a model to lift.

  2. 02Do those pages have an FAQ section using questions customers actually ask?

    Real questions in the customer's words, not headings you wrote for search engines.

  3. 03Does your site have structured data describing the business?

    JSON-LD in the page source describing who you are, what you do and where. If nobody has ever mentioned schema to you, the answer is probably no.

  4. 04Do your FAQs have FAQPage schema attached to them?

    Separate from having FAQs on the page. This is the markup that makes those answers machine-readable.

  5. 05Is your name, address and phone identical everywhere it appears online?

    Exactly identical — including whether you write Suite or Ste, and which phone number you use. Small inconsistencies are the common failure.

  6. 06Is your Google Business Profile claimed, complete and actively maintained?

    Claimed is the low bar. Complete means categories, services, hours, photos and posts — and updated within the last few months.

  7. 07Are you listed and current on the main directories for your industry?

    The ones specific to your field, not general business listings. If you cannot name them, that itself is the answer.

  8. 08Do you have recent reviews that mention specific services by name?

    A review saying "great experience" corroborates nothing. One naming the actual service tells a model what you do.

  9. 09Does your content include detail only someone in your market could write?

    Local specifics, your own process, real numbers. The test: could a competitor two states away publish the same page unchanged?

  10. 10Have your most important pages been reviewed in the last 12 months?

    Reviewed and updated where needed — not merely still online.

Questions about this tool

Does this actually check ChatGPT for my business?

No, and it says so throughout. This scores what you tell it about your own setup against the five factors that determine citability. Querying ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google for real is the free visibility check, which a person runs and walks you through.

Where do the answers go?

Nowhere, unless you ask us to send you the plan at the end. The scoring runs entirely in your browser and your answers are not transmitted while you work through the questions. There is no email gate on the result — you see the full breakdown whether or not you give us anything. Like most sites, this page loads analytics that records the visit itself; it cannot see what you selected.

What are the five factors based on?

How AI assistants actually assemble a recommendation: whether your page holds a liftable answer, whether your markup states your facts, whether independent sources corroborate them, whether you are present in the sources that matter in your category, and whether your content is specific enough to be worth citing.

What if I don't know the answer to a question?

Choose "No / not sure". For most of these, not knowing is functionally the same as no — if nobody has ever mentioned schema markup to you, it is almost certainly absent. Each question has a note explaining what it means in practice.

How long does it take?

Two or three minutes. Ten questions, three options each, and the result appears immediately with a per-factor breakdown and what to do about the weakest one.

The five factors are explained in full on the homepage, and applied per industry on the industry pages.

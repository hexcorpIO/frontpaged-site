12:T1e41,<h2 id="aesthetic-content-is-judged-more-harshly-than-most">Aesthetic Content Is Judged More Harshly Than Most</h2>
<p>Google does not apply one uniform quality bar to the whole web. Content that could affect a person&#39;s health, safety, or finances sits in a category the search quality guidelines call Your Money or Your Life, and it is evaluated far more strictly.</p>
<p>Med spa content lands squarely inside it. You are publishing about injectable neurotoxins, dermal fillers, energy-based devices, and prescription-strength skincare. A bad decision informed by a bad page has real consequences.</p>
<p>The practical result: a generic, unattributed, thinly-sourced article about Botox will struggle to rank no matter how well its keywords are placed. Meanwhile a thorough page attributed to a named nurse injector with visible credentials can outrank far larger sites.</p>
<p>E-E-A-T is the framework behind that difference, and most of it is within your control.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="experience-show-that-you-have-actually-done-this">Experience: Show That You Have Actually Done This</h2>
<p>The first E was added because Google noticed that firsthand experience matters independently of formal credentials.</p>
<p>For a med spa, experience is your greatest natural advantage. You treat patients every week. Almost nobody else writing about these treatments does.</p>
<p>Use it. Write about the questions patients ask in the room. Describe what the first twenty-four hours after a treatment actually feel like, based on what your patients report. Explain the tradeoffs you talk through during consultations.</p>
<p>Compare these two sentences:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Microneedling stimulates collagen production and improves skin texture over a series of treatments.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p>Most of our patients need three to four microneedling sessions spaced four weeks apart before they see the texture change they were hoping for. The first session almost always underwhelms people, which is why we explain the timeline before anyone books.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>The second could only have been written by someone who does this work. That is the signal.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="expertise-name-the-person-behind-the-content">Expertise: Name the Person Behind the Content</h2>
<p>Unattributed medical content is a persistent weakness across med spa sites. Posts appear under &quot;Admin&quot; or a generic team byline, or with no author at all.</p>
<p>Fix the chain of accountability:</p>
<ul>
<li>Give every clinical post a named author or a named reviewer.</li>
<li>Include their credential — RN, NP, PA-C, MD, DO.</li>
<li>Link the byline to a real bio page listing their training, licensure, and years in practice.</li>
<li>If a non-clinical writer drafted the piece, add an explicit line: &quot;Clinically reviewed by [name], [credential].&quot;</li>
</ul>
<p>That review line is the highest-value, lowest-effort change available to most practices. It takes one sentence and converts an anonymous article into an attributable one.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="authoritativeness-corroboration-from-elsewhere">Authoritativeness: Corroboration From Elsewhere</h2>
<p>Authority is what other sources say about you. It cannot be self-declared, which is precisely why it counts.</p>
<p>The signals that move it:</p>
<p><strong>Consistent listings.</strong> Your name, address, and phone number identical across your site, Google Business Profile, and the major aesthetic directories. Inconsistency here quietly undermines everything else.</p>
<p><strong>Legitimate citations.</strong> Link to primary sources — manufacturer prescribing information, professional bodies, peer-reviewed research — when you make a clinical claim. Linking out to authoritative sources does not leak value; it demonstrates that your claims have a basis.</p>
<p><strong>Local presence.</strong> Mentions in local press, &quot;best of&quot; roundups, community sponsorships. These are genuine third-party corroboration that you exist and matter in your market.</p>
<p><strong>Reviews with substance.</strong> A review naming a specific treatment is evidence. Our post on <a href="/blog/patient-reviews-and-visibility/">patient reviews and visibility</a> covers how to encourage the detailed kind.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="trustworthiness-the-foundation-everything-rests-on">Trustworthiness: The Foundation Everything Rests On</h2>
<p>Trust is the component Google weights most heavily for YMYL content, and it is largely operational rather than editorial.</p>
<p>Get the basics right:</p>
<ul>
<li>Real physical address and phone number, visible without hunting.</li>
<li>Named providers with verifiable credentials.</li>
<li>Clear, accurate pricing information.</li>
<li>A privacy policy that reflects what you actually do with patient data.</li>
<li>Working HTTPS with no mixed-content warnings.</li>
<li>Accurate current information — no pages describing a device you retired in 2024.</li>
</ul>
<p>Then avoid the things that destroy trust fastest: guaranteed outcomes, before-and-after images that misrepresent typical results, invented statistics, and fake urgency. Beyond the search consequences, outcome guarantees in aesthetic advertising carry real regulatory exposure.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="what-this-looks-like-on-a-page">What This Looks Like on a Page</h2>
<p>Take a typical Botox page and apply the framework:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Experience</strong> — a paragraph on what patients typically report during the first week, drawn from your own practice.</li>
<li><strong>Expertise</strong> — a byline naming the injector, with credential and a link to their bio.</li>
<li><strong>Authoritativeness</strong> — a citation to the manufacturer&#39;s prescribing information where you state how long results last.</li>
<li><strong>Trustworthiness</strong> — an honest cost range, a clear statement of who is not a good candidate, and a last-updated date.</li>
</ul>
<p>None of that is exotic. It is mostly a matter of deciding to do it, then doing it consistently across the site rather than on one showcase page.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="why-ai-assistants-care-even-more">Why AI Assistants Care Even More</h2>
<p>Traditional search can hedge by returning ten results and letting the user judge. An AI assistant answering &quot;is Morpheus8 safe&quot; gives one answer, and whoever it draws from carries real weight.</p>
<p>That raises the bar on source selection. Models favor content that is attributed, internally consistent, corroborated elsewhere, and free of the exaggeration patterns that mark promotional copy. E-E-A-T signals are close to a direct description of what makes a source citable.</p>
<p>This is the same underlying logic we described in <a href="/blog/how-ai-search-recommends-med-spas/">how AI search recommends med spas</a>, viewed from the trust side rather than the structure side. Getting the structure right makes you extractable. Getting E-E-A-T right makes you worth extracting.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="an-audit-you-can-run-this-week">An Audit You Can Run This Week</h2>
<p>Open your five most important pages and check each one:</p>
<ol>
<li>Is there a named author or reviewer with a credential?</li>
<li>Does it contain at least one detail that could only come from treating patients?</li>
<li>Are clinical claims sourced?</li>
<li>Is contact information and pricing accurate today?</li>
<li>Does it promise any outcome it cannot guarantee?</li>
</ol>
<p>Anything failing question five is urgent. Everything else is a straightforward afternoon&#39;s work with a real return.</p>

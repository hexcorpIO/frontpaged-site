14:T203c,<h2 id="the-result-above-the-results">The Result Above the Results</h2>
<p>Search &quot;how long does lip filler last&quot; or &quot;is Morpheus8 worth it&quot; and you will often see a generated summary before any blue links, assembled from several sources with citations beside it.</p>
<p>That is an AI Overview, and for a growing share of aesthetic queries it is the first thing a patient reads. Sometimes it is the only thing.</p>
<p>This changes the target. Ranking third used to mean a reasonable share of clicks. Now, if positions one, four, and nine are cited in the overview and you are third but uncited, you may be less visible than a page ranked below you.</p>
<p>The good news: unlike the single featured snippet, an overview cites several sources. There is room for more than one winner.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="ranking-is-still-the-entry-requirement">Ranking Is Still the Entry Requirement</h2>
<p>The most useful thing to understand about AI Overviews is that they are not a separate system you can optimize independently.</p>
<p>Google assembles them largely from pages already performing well for the query. Pages on page four do not get cited. So conventional SEO — relevance, authority, technical health, local signals — remains the price of admission.</p>
<p>What changes is what happens <em>after</em> you rank. Among pages that all rank respectably, the ones that get cited are the ones structured so a passage can be lifted cleanly and attributed confidently.</p>
<p>That is the whole difference. Ranking gets you considered. Structure gets you cited.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="what-makes-a-passage-citable">What Makes a Passage Citable</h2>
<p>Look at the pages cited in overviews for aesthetic queries and a consistent shape appears.</p>
<p><strong>The answer arrives immediately.</strong> Within one or two sentences of a heading, not four paragraphs into a narrative.</p>
<p><strong>The passage stands alone.</strong> It makes sense lifted out of context, without depending on what came before. Sections opening with &quot;As we mentioned above&quot; are difficult to extract.</p>
<p><strong>It is specific.</strong> &quot;Lip filler typically lasts six to twelve months depending on the product and the individual&#39;s metabolism&quot; is citable. &quot;Results vary from patient to patient&quot; is not.</p>
<p><strong>It is hedged appropriately.</strong> Overconfident claims about medical outcomes get filtered. &quot;Typically,&quot; &quot;most patients,&quot; &quot;depending on&quot; are features, not weaknesses.</p>
<p><strong>It is attributable.</strong> Content from a named credentialed provider on a site with real business information is a safer source than an anonymous page. This is where <a href="/blog/eeat-for-med-spas/">E-E-A-T</a> does its work.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="structuring-a-page-to-qualify">Structuring a Page to Qualify</h2>
<p>The practical translation is straightforward.</p>
<p>Use question-shaped headings. &quot;How long does lip filler last?&quot; rather than &quot;Longevity.&quot; The heading gives the engine an explicit signal about which question the section answers.</p>
<p>Answer directly beneath the heading. First sentence, no preamble.</p>
<p>Keep paragraphs short. Two to four sentences. Long paragraphs bury the extractable part inside material the engine must discard.</p>
<p>Use lists where the content is genuinely a list — steps, candidacy criteria, aftercare instructions. Structured formatting is easier to parse than the same content in prose.</p>
<p>Add an FAQ block with schema so questions and answers are explicitly machine-readable. Our <a href="/blog/faq-schema-for-med-spas/">FAQ schema guide</a> covers implementation.</p>
<p>None of this is new to anyone who has read our <a href="/blog/med-spa-service-page-template/">service page template</a>. Overviews reward the same structure that already served featured snippets and human readers.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="a-worked-example">A Worked Example</h2>
<p>Take the query &quot;how much does lip filler cost,&quot; which nearly every practice wants to appear for.</p>
<p>Here is how most med spa pages handle it:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Pricing for our lip enhancement treatments varies depending on your individual goals, the products selected, and the treatment plan developed during your personalized consultation. Contact us today to learn more about our competitive pricing.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>There is nothing in that paragraph to extract. It contains no number, no range, and no explanation of what drives cost. An engine assembling an answer about lip filler pricing has to look elsewhere.</p>
<p>Here is a version that qualifies:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Lip filler typically costs between $600 and $900 per syringe in most U.S. markets. Most first-time patients need one syringe, though some choose two for fuller results. The main factors affecting price are the product used, the injector&#39;s experience, and your local market. Because hyaluronic acid filler dissolves gradually, most patients return every nine to twelve months.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Four sentences, a concrete range, the factors that move it, and a follow-up detail about longevity. Every clause is liftable, and the hedging is honest rather than evasive — &quot;typically,&quot; &quot;in most U.S. markets,&quot; &quot;some choose.&quot;</p>
<p>Notice that the second version does not commit the practice to a fixed price. Addressing cost and publishing a rigid price list are different things, and the fear of the second is what keeps most practices from doing the first.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="local-queries-behave-differently">Local Queries Behave Differently</h2>
<p>For &quot;best med spa in [city],&quot; Google is less likely to generate a purely informational overview and more likely to lean on local results — the map pack, reviews, and Business Profile data.</p>
<p>For those queries the levers are different: a complete Google Business Profile, consistent listings, a healthy volume of recent detailed reviews, and clear location signals on your site.</p>
<p>Which means the two halves of your visibility work are complementary rather than competing. Informational content earns overview citations for treatment questions. Local signals win the provider-selection searches. Both are needed; neither substitutes for the other.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="measuring-something-google-doesnt-report">Measuring Something Google Doesn&#39;t Report</h2>
<p>Search Console does not break out AI Overview citations, which makes measurement manual for now.</p>
<p>A workable approach:</p>
<ol>
<li>List your fifteen most important queries — treatments, comparisons, cost questions, and local terms.</li>
<li>Once a month, from a logged-out browser located in your service area, run each one.</li>
<li>Record whether an overview appeared, which practices were cited, and whether you were among them.</li>
</ol>
<p>It takes half an hour a month and gives you a real baseline. Directional trends are what matter, not precision.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="what-not-to-do">What Not to Do</h2>
<p>Do not write pages purely for extraction. Pages that are nothing but disconnected question-and-answer fragments read poorly to humans, and human engagement still feeds the ranking that qualifies you in the first place.</p>
<p>Do not fabricate specificity. Making up a statistic to sound citable is the fastest way to lose trust with both patients and engines, and in medical content it carries real exposure.</p>
<p>Do not abandon depth. Overviews cite passages, but they cite passages from pages that demonstrate genuine command of the subject.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="start-with-five-queries">Start With Five Queries</h2>
<p>Pick the five questions your patients ask most. Search each one and read the overview if there is one.</p>
<p>Then look at your own page for that question. Is there a heading matching it? Does the answer arrive in the first sentence beneath? Could that passage be lifted and stand on its own?</p>
<p>Where the answer is no, you have found the work.</p>

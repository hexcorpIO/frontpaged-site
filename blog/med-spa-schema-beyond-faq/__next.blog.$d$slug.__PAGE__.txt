13:T2139,<h2 id="structured-data-is-how-you-stop-being-guessed-about">Structured Data Is How You Stop Being Guessed About</h2>
<p>Search engines read your pages as text and infer meaning. Structured data replaces inference with declaration.</p>
<p>Without it, Google works out that you are a med spa in Nashville by reading your copy and cross-referencing your listings. It usually gets there. But &quot;usually&quot; leaves room for error, and errors compound in local search where the difference between appearing in the map pack and not is often a matter of clarity.</p>
<p>With schema, you state it outright, in a format built for machines: this is a medical business, at this address, offering these services, in this area, with these hours, at this price range.</p>
<p>Most med spas that have implemented any schema have implemented FAQ markup and stopped. Our <a href="/blog/faq-schema-for-med-spas/">FAQ schema guide</a> covers that piece. This post covers the rest.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="the-business-node">The Business Node</h2>
<p>The foundation is a single node describing the practice itself, present on every page.</p>
<p>For most med spas the right type is <code>MedicalBusiness</code>, since treatments are performed by licensed staff under medical supervision. Practices offering purely cosmetic services without clinical oversight are better described as <code>LocalBusiness</code> with a <code>HealthAndBeautyBusiness</code> subtype.</p>
<p>Whichever you choose, include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Legal business name, exactly as it appears on your Google Business Profile</li>
<li>Full street address</li>
<li>Phone number in international format</li>
<li>Opening hours</li>
<li>Price range</li>
<li>Latitude and longitude</li>
<li><code>sameAs</code> links to every profile you control — Google Business Profile, Instagram, LinkedIn, manufacturer locator listings</li>
<li><code>areaServed</code> covering the cities and neighborhoods you actually draw from</li>
</ul>
<p>Two details matter more than they appear. First, <code>sameAs</code> is how you connect your website to your other profiles, which is what allows an engine to treat them as one entity rather than several similarly-named businesses. Second, every value here must match your other listings character for character. Schema that disagrees with your Google Business Profile creates precisely the ambiguity it was meant to remove.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="service-markup-on-treatment-pages">Service Markup on Treatment Pages</h2>
<p>Every treatment page should carry a <code>Service</code> node naming the treatment, the provider, and the area served.</p>
<p>Where you publish pricing, include an <code>Offer</code> with a price or price range and currency. This is one of the more valuable pieces of markup available to a med spa, because cost is among the highest-volume question patterns in aesthetics, and structured pricing is unambiguous in a way prose is not.</p>
<p>Keep the markup honest. If your page says treatments start at $400 and your schema says $250, you have created a contradiction that undermines both. Schema describes the page; it does not get to describe a better version of the page.</p>
<p>Link the service to the business node by reference rather than repeating the business details on every page. One canonical business definition, referenced everywhere, keeps the graph consistent.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="breadcrumbs-and-articles">Breadcrumbs and Articles</h2>
<p><code>BreadcrumbList</code> markup tells engines where a page sits in your hierarchy. It is inexpensive, it produces the navigational path shown under search results, and it helps establish that your service pages form a structured group rather than a flat pile.</p>
<p>Blog content should carry <code>Article</code> or <code>BlogPosting</code> markup with headline, description, publication and modification dates, author, publisher, and image. The author field is where structured data and <a href="/blog/eeat-for-med-spas/">E-E-A-T</a> meet: naming a credentialed provider in your markup makes the attribution machine-readable rather than merely visible.</p>
<p>Keep <code>dateModified</code> accurate. It is one of the clearest freshness signals available, and it is meaningless if it updates on every deploy regardless of whether anything changed.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="why-this-matters-more-for-ai-search">Why This Matters More for AI Search</h2>
<p>Traditional search can tolerate ambiguity because it returns ten results and lets the user sort it out.</p>
<p>An AI assistant answering &quot;what&#39;s a good med spa in Nashville for Morpheus8&quot; has to commit. To include you, it needs confidence about several things at once: that you exist as a business, that you are located where the user is asking about, that you offer that specific treatment, and that the source describing all this is reliable.</p>
<p>Structured data answers every one of those questions directly. It is the difference between a model inferring your business from marketing prose and reading a declaration of fact.</p>
<p>This is the same argument we made in <a href="/blog/what-is-generative-engine-optimization/">what generative engine optimization is</a>, applied to the technical layer. Content structure makes your answers extractable. Schema makes your business unambiguous.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="the-mistakes-that-cause-real-damage">The Mistakes That Cause Real Damage</h2>
<p>Most schema problems fall into a handful of patterns, and a few carry genuine consequences.</p>
<p><strong>Marking up content that is not on the page.</strong> The most common serious error. A plugin adds FAQ markup containing questions that appear nowhere in the visible content, or service markup for treatments the page does not describe. This is an explicit structured data violation and can trigger a manual action that removes rich result eligibility across the site.</p>
<p><strong>Multiple conflicting business nodes.</strong> A theme emits one <code>LocalBusiness</code>, a plugin emits another, and the two disagree about hours or phone number. Engines now have to choose which to believe. Audit what your site actually outputs rather than what you think you configured.</p>
<p><strong>Stale prices.</strong> Schema saying $400 when the page says $550 is worse than no pricing markup at all, because it contradicts a source the engine can also read.</p>
<p><strong>Fabricated review markup.</strong> Self-serving <code>AggregateRating</code> on your own pages — particularly ratings not backed by genuinely collected reviews — is among the fastest ways to attract a penalty. Let review platforms carry review data.</p>
<p><strong>Markup on the wrong page.</strong> Article schema on a service page, or service schema on a blog post, confuses rather than clarifies.</p>
<p>The governing principle is that structured data describes reality. Every time you are tempted to have it describe something slightly better than reality, you are trading a small short-term gain for a real risk.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="validating-what-you-deploy">Validating What You Deploy</h2>
<p>Never trust markup you have not tested.</p>
<p>Google&#39;s Rich Results Test shows what Google can parse and which rich results you qualify for. The Schema.org validator catches structural errors the Google tool ignores. Search Console&#39;s enhancement reports surface problems across your whole site over time.</p>
<p>Run a page through all three after any template change. Plugin-generated markup in particular tends to be adequate for business and article types and weak for service markup, so verify rather than assume.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="a-sensible-implementation-order">A Sensible Implementation Order</h2>
<p>If you are starting from FAQ markup only:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Business node sitewide.</strong> Biggest single gain, one implementation.</li>
<li><strong>Service markup on your top three revenue treatments.</strong> Include offers where you publish pricing.</li>
<li><strong>Breadcrumbs.</strong> Cheap and immediately useful.</li>
<li><strong>Article markup on blog content,</strong> with named credentialed authors.</li>
<li><strong>Remaining service pages,</strong> worked through by revenue.</li>
</ol>
<p>Then set a calendar reminder to audit everything in six months, because the markup will drift as the business changes.</p>

## The Page Everyone Builds in December and Deletes in January

The pattern repeats every year. In the last week of November, a practice realizes the holidays are here and publishes a page announcing a seasonal package. It gets shared on social media, does modest business, and is deleted in early January.

Twelve months later the same practice builds the same page from scratch, at a new URL, with no accumulated authority.

Two things go wrong. First, a page published in late November will not rank organically before the season ends — pages need weeks to gain traction, and by the time this one might have, the offer has expired. Second, deleting it each January guarantees you begin from zero every single year.

A permanent seasonal URL, updated annually, compounds. Five years of the same well-maintained page is a genuinely strong asset. Five separate pages built and destroyed is nothing.

---

## What Actually Spikes in Q4

Before planning offers, it is worth knowing what people search for.

**Gift cards.** By a wide margin the largest seasonal search category in aesthetics. "Med spa gift card," "Botox gift certificate," "spa gift card near me." Volume climbs through November and peaks in the ten days before Christmas.

Most practices handle this badly. If your gift cards require a phone call during business hours, you are losing every buyer shopping at 10pm on December 22nd. Online purchase with instant delivery is the single highest-return holiday improvement available to most med spas.

**Event timing.** "How long before an event should I get Botox," "filler before a wedding," "how far in advance for a chemical peel." These spike ahead of holiday parties and are among the most useful questions you can answer, since the honest answer — leave two weeks — protects patients from a bad outcome and positions you as trustworthy.

**Last-minute availability.** "Botox appointment this week." High intent, and largely won through your Google Business Profile rather than your website.

**New year planning.** Beginning around December 26th, searches shift toward longer-horizon treatments — skin resurfacing, body contouring, treatment plans. This is a distinct season inside the season.

Notice that only one of those categories is about discounts.

## Structure the Seasonal Content

Build a small permanent cluster rather than one disposable page.

**A permanent offers page** at a stable URL like `/specials/` or `/offers/`. Update the content seasonally; never change the URL. Between promotions, keep it live with your standing packages rather than leaving it advertising an expired deal.

**A gift card page**, permanent, with online purchase. This should exist year-round and simply become more prominent in November.

**Seasonal question posts** covering the timing questions above. These are evergreen — "how long before an event should I get Botox" is asked in June too — and they feed traffic into the offers page.

Link the cluster together and link from the offers page to the relevant service pages, so seasonal traffic reaches the pages built to convert it.

---

## Timing the Publication

Work backwards from when you need traffic.

- **Early October:** publish or refresh the offers page and the seasonal question posts. Even without final pricing, get the page live and updateable.
- **Late October:** make the gift card page prominent in navigation.
- **Early November:** finalize offers on the existing page. Begin Google Business Profile posts.
- **Late November through December:** promotion, social, email. The organic work is already done by this point.
- **Late December:** shift copy toward new-year treatment planning.
- **January:** update the offers page to standing packages. Do not delete it.

The critical constraint is that organic ranking cannot be bought at the last minute. Everything published after mid-November is functionally a paid and social play.

## Google Business Profile Is Where Last-Minute Traffic Lands

Someone searching "med spa open Saturday near me" on December 21st is not reading your blog. They are looking at the map pack.

Through Q4, keep your profile current: holiday hours entered well in advance, gift cards listed as a product or service, weekly posts about the seasonal offer, and fast responses to messages. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-for-med-spas) covers the underlying configuration.

Holiday hours in particular are worth care. A patient who drives to a closed clinic because your listing said you were open writes a review that outlasts the season by years.

## Offer Design That Doesn't Damage You

Discounting is the reflex, and it is usually the weakest option.

Deep discounts attract price-driven patients who move to whoever is cheapest next quarter, and they train your existing patients to wait for sales rather than book at full price.

Alternatives that convert comparably without the damage:

- **Gift card bonuses.** Spend $500, receive $100 extra. Costs less than a straight discount and brings a new person into your practice.
- **Packages.** Three sessions at a modest saving. Increases commitment and lifetime value.
- **Value adds.** A complimentary treatment alongside a booked service. Introduces patients to something they may buy again.
- **Pre-purchase for January.** Sells now, delivers in your quietest month.

That last one is genuinely underused. January is slow in most practices, and selling December revenue against January capacity solves both problems at once.

Whichever you choose, put an end date on it and honour that date. Offers that quietly run forever stop being offers, and patients learn that your published prices are not real prices.

---

## Plan the Refresh Now

Whatever you build this year, put a calendar reminder for the first week of October next year to refresh it rather than rebuild it. That single habit is the difference between a page that compounds and a page that resets.

The same logic applies to the rest of your seasonal assets. Your gift card page, your timing posts, and your offers page all want annual updates rather than annual replacement. Treat the URLs as permanent infrastructure and the content inside them as the thing that changes.

## The Template Trap

Here is the approach that fails, and it fails reliably.

A practice with four locations builds one page template. Each version substitutes the city name into the same paragraphs. "Our Brentwood med spa offers Botox, filler, and laser treatments in a luxurious setting." Then Franklin. Then Murfreesboro.

Four pages that are ninety-five percent identical. Search engines recognize this instantly — it is one of the oldest patterns in local SEO — and typically pick one to rank while ignoring the rest, or ignore all of them.

The instinct behind it is sound. You genuinely do serve those areas, and you genuinely want to appear in searches there. The execution is what fails, because a page whose only unique content is a city name contains no information a patient could not get from your homepage.

---

## What Makes a Location Page Legitimate

The test is straightforward: does this page contain information that exists nowhere else on the site?

For a real location, plenty does.

**The address and directions.** Not just the street address — the landmark it sits behind, which side of the road, where to park, whether the entrance is round the back. Patients genuinely need this, and no other page has it.

**Hours for that location.** Which often differ.

**The staff who work there.** Named, with credentials and photographs. This is the strongest differentiator available and the one most often skipped. Patients choose providers, not buildings.

**Services actually offered there.** Device-based treatments in particular are rarely available at every site, and telling someone the wrong location has their Morpheus8 device is a real service failure.

**That location's phone number,** if it has one.

**Reviews from that location's Google Business Profile.**

**Genuine neighborhood context.** Not "conveniently located in the heart of beautiful Franklin" — something a local would recognize as true.

Assemble those and the page writes itself, because you are describing something real rather than dressing up a template.

## One Profile Per Location

The website is only half of it. Each staffed location needs its own verified Google Business Profile.

Get these right:

- Each profile's website field points at that location's page, not your homepage. This is the link that ties a location to its geography.
- Name, address, and phone are identical across the profile, the page, and your schema markup. Our post on [schema beyond FAQ markup](/blog/med-spa-schema-beyond-faq) covers emitting per-location business data correctly.
- Each profile lists the services actually available there.
- Reviews are gathered per location rather than all funneled to the flagship.

Practices routinely lose local visibility because three locations share one profile, or because the profiles all point at the homepage. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-for-med-spas) covers configuration in detail.

## Structuring the Section

Keep the hierarchy shallow and obvious.

A `/locations/` index listing every location with address, hours, and a link. Then one page per location beneath it. Each location page links back to the index and out to the service pages for treatments offered there.

Where a location has a treatment that is genuinely a local specialty — the only site with a particular device, say — a location-plus-treatment page can be justified. But only where the content is substantively different. Building the full cross-product of four locations by fifteen treatments regenerates the template trap at larger scale.

## Serving an Area Without an Office There

This is the honest hard case. You draw patients from a suburb thirty minutes away and want to appear when they search.

You cannot claim a Google Business Profile there — that requires a real staffed address, and fabricating one is a suspension risk that occasionally ends in permanent removal.

What you can do legitimately:

- Write genuinely useful content about serving that area: how long the drive takes, where patients park, why people from there come to you.
- Earn local mentions — sponsorships, press, community involvement in that area.
- Collect reviews from patients who live there, since review text mentioning a place is a real relevance signal you do not control and therefore one that counts.

This is slower than spinning up a page, and it is the only approach that survives contact with an algorithm update.

## What a Real Location Page Reads Like

The difference between a template page and a real one is easiest to see side by side.

The template version:

> **Med Spa in Franklin**
>
> Our Franklin med spa offers a full range of aesthetic treatments including Botox, dermal fillers, laser hair removal, and medical-grade facials. Our experienced team is dedicated to helping you look and feel your best in a luxurious, welcoming environment. Contact our Franklin location today to schedule your consultation.

Swap "Franklin" for any other town and the paragraph remains equally true and equally useless.

The real version opens differently:

> **Med Spa in Franklin — Cool Springs**
>
> Our Franklin location is on the second floor of the Mallory Lane medical building, behind the Cool Springs Galleria. Parking is free in the rear lot; the elevator is by the north entrance.
>
> Franklin is our only location with the Morpheus8 device, so patients from our Brentwood and Murfreesboro offices are usually booked here for RF microneedling.
>
> **Hours:** Monday–Thursday 9–6, Friday 9–4, closed weekends.
>
> **Your injectors here:** Sarah Chen, NP-C, who has led this location since it opened in 2023 and specializes in lip filler, and Marcus Webb, PA-C, who splits his week between Franklin and Brentwood.

Every sentence contains something that is only true of Franklin. It is more useful to a patient, impossible to duplicate, and it gives search engines and AI assistants concrete facts to attach to that geography.

It also took no longer to write. It just required describing the actual place instead of a generic one.

## Measuring Per Location

Aggregate reporting hides location-level problems. Segment everything.

In Search Console, filter performance by each location page's URL. In Google Business Profile, review each profile's insights separately. Track calls, direction requests, and bookings by location.

The common finding is one location quietly underperforming — usually the newest, sometimes one with a thin profile or few reviews. That is invisible in a site-wide chart and obvious the moment you segment.

---

## Where to Start

Open your location pages side by side and read them as a patient would. If you can swap the city names without the pages becoming wrong, they are not yet doing their job.

Pick your weakest-performing location. Add the staff, the parking, the real hours, the services actually offered there. Then check its Google Business Profile points at that page.
